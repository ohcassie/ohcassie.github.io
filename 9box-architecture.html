9-Box Talent Review Redesign

Turning a PDF-based 9-box that couldn't be aggregated into a fixed-schema Excel model that rolls up cleanly, still adapts across functions, and keeps subjective ratings from drifting.

In production, continuously iterated

DWG-01 · Site Survey

A subjective exercise, trapped in a PDF

The first version, built in 2024, was a standard 9-box template not unlike what you'd find in any generic search: a fillable PDF. It did the job for a single leader filling out a single form. It fell apart the moment senior leadership needed to see the whole organization at once.

01

No fixed schema. Every PDF was its own island, so rolling individual forms up into an org-wide view meant manually pulling data out of dozens of documents.

02

Genuinely subjective, and new. Senior leaders hadn't rated performance and potential this way before, so ratings varied widely from leader to leader.

03

Heavy hand-holding required. HRBPs had to walk leaders through lengthy job aids defining every rating just to get consistent input.

DWG-02 · Structural Plan

What changed when it moved to Excel

With no dedicated talent review software to build on, the fix wasn't a new system. It was a spreadsheet designed to behave like one.

01

Fixed schema

Every leader fills out the same structure, so individual sheets can be aggregated into an org-wide roll-up without manual reformatting.

02

Dropdown scale for functional lenses

Different functions read "performance" and "potential" differently. A dropdown lets the scale flex to the right lens without breaking the underlying schema.

03

Auto-generated 9-box matrix

Once ratings are entered, the matrix builds itself. No one is manually plotting dots or redrawing a grid for leadership review.

04

Data validation on ratings

Every input is checked to be numeric, closing off the stray text and inconsistent entries that made the PDF version hard to trust.

05

Automatic Hi-Po flag

When a colleague lands in the top-right of the grid, high performance and high potential, the sheet flags them automatically, rather than relying on someone to notice. That flag is also what triggers backend calibration with HRBPs when needed.

DWG-03 · The Eight Questions

Two tabs, four questions each

Rather than one subjective gut-check number per axis, each leader answers four specific questions per tab, each on a 1-5 scale. The two averages are what actually place a colleague on the grid.

Performance · 01

Consistently meets role expectations

Performance · 02

Quality and accuracy of work

Performance · 03

Accountability for results

Performance · 04

Delivers results with appropriate independence

Potential · 01

Learning agility and skill acquisition

Potential · 02

Readiness for expanded scope or responsibility

Potential · 03

Handles increased complexity and ambiguity

Potential · 04

Influence beyond role and leadership behaviors

DWG-05 · The Roll-Up

Closing the loop the PDF version never could

The fixed schema only pays off if someone can actually turn dozens of individual sheets into one picture. Once HRBPs return their completed sheets, a script pulls the data together and compiles it into a high-level summary for the CHRO, instead of someone manually reading through each one.

01

Self-taught. Learned to use Python in Excel with a bit of help from Copilot, specifically to solve this aggregation problem.

02

Built on the schema. The script only works because every leader's sheet follows the same fixed structure from DWG-02.

03

CHRO-ready output. Turns individual leader submissions into one high-level organizational summary, on demand.

DWG-06 · Sign-Off

Where it stands

The Excel model is live and in active use. It continues to iterate as new functional lenses and edge cases surface, the same way the performance management framework did before it launched.
