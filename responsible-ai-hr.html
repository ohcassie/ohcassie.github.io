DWG-01 · Site Survey

Speed without a standard is how AI gets HR in trouble

None of the AI work on this site happened because there was nothing to worry about. It happened because usefulness and risk were being weighed deliberately, use case by use case, before anything shipped. But that judgment call was living entirely in one person's head. It needed to be a written standard instead, one that says the same thing regardless of who's asking or how confident the tool sounds.

HR sits on some of the most consequential and most protected data in the company: pay, performance, medical accommodations, terminations. An agent that's genuinely useful for answering a PTO question is the same category of tool someone might be tempted to point at a promotion decision. The framework exists to draw that line before it gets crossed by accident.

01

AI tools were shipping faster than there was a shared, written answer to "what should this thing never decide on its own."

02

Human-in-the-loop expectations varied by tool and by builder instinct, not by a consistent standard applied the same way every time.

03

HR data reflects real historical inequities. An agent used carelessly, especially in screening or ranking, can quietly reproduce them at scale.

04

Without a record of what the agent saw, recommended, or did versus what a person ultimately decided, a contested call had nothing to point back to.

DWG-02 · Structural Plan

Three tiers, one rule underneath all of them

Every AI use case in HR gets sorted into exactly one of three tiers before it's built, not after. The test is simple: how reversible is this, and whose life does it change if the agent gets it wrong. Click a tier to open it.

DWG-04 · Applied

Not theoretical. Already applied to what's live.

The framework isn't a slide that lives separately from the work. Here's how it maps onto the AI tools already built.

ToolBehaviorTier
Development / IDP BotAnswers career development questions directlyTier 1
HR Policies & FAQ BotAnswers policy and process questions directlyTier 1
New Manager Support BotCoaches a leader through a PIP or performance conversationTier 2
Onboarding AgentReminds a manager a check-in isn't scheduled yetTier 1
Onboarding AgentEscalates a still-missed check-in to an HR partnerTier 2

Notice what's absent: nothing here decides a rating, a promotion, or a paycheck on its own. That's not an accident, it's the framework working as intended.

DWG-05 · Sign-Off

Where it stands

Adopted as the internal standard for this work: applied retroactively to the three live chatbots, and built into the onboarding agent's design from day one rather than bolted on after.

DONE

Classify every existing AI tool

Sorted the chatbots and the onboarding agent's behaviors into tiers, and confirmed nothing already live was quietly operating in Tier 3 territory.

NEXT

Build the logging requirement into the platform, not just the doc

Add the "always logged" pillar directly into the Copilot Studio build pattern, so it's structural rather than something a builder has to remember.

THEN

Make it the first question for every new AI idea

Any new use case gets classified before it gets scoped, not after a prototype already exists and momentum makes Tier 3 feel negotiable.
