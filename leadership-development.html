How I Think About Leadership Development

A one-size-fits-all training calendar doesn't change how anyone leads. My approach starts with what the business actually needs from its leaders at each level, then builds backward to the program, not the other way around. DWG-01 · Philosophy

Leadership development fails when it's generic

Most leadership training gets built around a catalog of topics that sound right in the abstract: communication, delegation, feedback. What it usually skips is the harder question underneath: which behaviors does this specific business actually need more of right now, and from which leaders. Skip that question and you get a well-produced program nobody's behavior actually changes.

The other place generic programs fail is treating every leader the same. A first-time manager six months into their first direct report is solving a completely different problem than someone leading a team of managers. Same word, "leadership," two different jobs. A program that tries to serve both at once usually serves neither well.

"Start from the org's real goals and the specific gap in front of a specific group of leaders. Build the program to close that gap. Everything else, the modules, the cadence, the format, is a means to that end, not the point of the exercise."

DWG-02 · Structural Plan

DWG-03 · Working Model

Two audiences, two different programs

The program splits into two tracks rather than one shared curriculum, because a brand-new manager and someone leading other managers are solving genuinely different problems. Pick a track to see what it actually focuses on.

DWG-04 · Structural Plan

How the program actually got built

The same sequence every time, regardless of which track: understand the real gap before designing a single session.

01

Assess the current state

Reviewed organizational and HR strategy, current leadership competencies, and where the real gaps sat, rather than starting from a list of generic leadership topics.

02

Define the priorities and objectives

Turned the gap analysis into a short list of leadership priorities and concrete learning objectives for each track, so every session could be tied back to a specific behavior change.

03

Build the program

Mapped the learning experience across all three channels, formal sessions, coaching structures, and on-the-job application, instead of defaulting to a training-only build.

04

Measure and iterate

Defined how effectiveness would actually be tracked before launch, then used real cohort feedback and outcomes to adjust the next run rather than treating v1 as final.
